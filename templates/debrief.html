Scene 06 · The levers
  • URGENCY
  • FEAR

Every attack pulls one of these levers. The popup, the countdown, the "grandma" text — all engineered to bypass the part of your brain that checks.

Scene 06 · The signs

Things you missed.

Sender 01

The domain had a typo

"bankofsecuure.com" with a double-U. Registered by an attacker to catch fast readers.

Transport 02

The URL was HTTP, not HTTPS

No padlock, no TLS. Real banks redirect you to HTTPS the moment you touch login. A raw http:// at a bank URL is a dead giveaway.

UI 03

A popup asked for your password

Real sessions don't expire inside a popup. When your session ends, the bank sends you to the login page — not an overlay with a countdown.

Timing 04

Everything was urgent

"ACT NOW". "08 seconds". "Immediate verification". Urgency is the single most reliable signal of an attack — it's designed to skip your judgment.

Scene 07 · Next time

What to do
instead.

01
Read the sender, not the brand The display name means nothing. The @ domain is the only part that's verifiable.
02
Check the URL before you type Read every character, left to right. A lookalike letter or extra hyphen is the attacker's signature.
03
Never trust a popup with your data If an overlay asks for SSN, password, or a 2FA code — close the tab. Go to the site directly.
04
When it feels urgent, slow down Urgency is the lure. Any message that won't survive five seconds of calm review is almost certainly an attack.
